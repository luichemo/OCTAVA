# Product Marketing Context

**Document version:** v1
**Last updated:** 2026-10-20

## Product Overview
**One-liner:** Swipe. Jam. Form a band. (A swipe app for musicians: hear people play, match, chat and form a band.)
**What it does:** Musicians make a profile with short audio clips, then swipe through other musicians nearby: Jam on the ones they'd like to play with, Pass on the rest. When both choose Jam it's a match and they can chat (text and voice). Matches can form a band together, and bands appear as cards in the feed so other musicians can ask to join; bands then get shared tools (rehearsal calendar, songs, setlists, a public band page).
**Product category:** Find bandmates / musician matching app ("Tinder for musicians"). People search for: find bandmates, find musicians near me, join a band, musicians wanted, looking for a drummer/bassist/singer.
**Product type:** Consumer mobile app (iOS and Android; a web preview for testers). Two-sided network of musicians and bands.
**Business model:** Free. No subscriptions, no ads, no payments for now. Later (not before ~1,000 active users or Supabase costs over ~$50/month): an optional **OCTAVA Plus** subscription (~$3–5/month: see who Jammed you, more and longer clips, Undo on any swipe, wider filters, more avatar generations). Matching and chat stay free forever. Ads are ruled out.

## Target Audience
**Target users:** Musicians aged 16+, **worldwide from day one** (owner's choice: no single-city rollout), UI in English and Georgian. Marketing leads in English, with Georgian for Georgia. **First 100 users: hobbyists and beginners** (owner's choice) — they're the largest group and want fun and people at their level.
**Who decides:** The individual musician; for bands, the members together (every member approves a new member).
**Primary use case:** Finding people to make music with who fit on instrument, genre, skill, goals and distance, without cold-posting in groups and waiting.
**Jobs to be done:**
- "Help me find a drummer/bassist/singer who fits my band, nearby, and actually good enough."
- "Help me find a band, or people to start one, at my level and in my style."
- "Let me hear how someone plays before I spend an evening on a rehearsal with them."
**Use cases:**
- A guitarist who just moved to a city and knows no other musicians
- An existing band replacing a member who left
- A teenager (16–17) who wants to start or join a band safely
- A hobbyist who wants a weekly jam, not a career
- A working musician looking for gigging or session partners
- A band organising rehearsals, songs and setlists in one place

## Problems & Pain Points
**Core problem:** Finding the right people to play with is slow and random: you post in a Facebook group or forum, wait, get a few replies, and only find out at the first rehearsal whether the person can play your style.
**Why alternatives fall short:**
- Facebook groups, forums and classifieds: posts get buried, no filters, no way to hear someone first
- Word of mouth and jam nights: limited to who you already know
- Existing musician-finder sites: often dated, web-first, pay-to-contact, or empty in smaller cities (to verify per market)
**What it costs them:** Months without a band, wasted evenings on rehearsals that don't fit, bands that stall because one role stays empty.
**Emotional tension:** Awkwardness of cold-messaging strangers, fear of rejection or of not being good enough, worry about meeting strangers (especially for teens and their parents).

## Competitive Landscape
**Direct:** Other swipe/match apps for musicians (e.g. Vampr) and musician-finder sites (e.g. BandMix, JoinMyBand) — falls short because: to research; likely weaker in Georgian-speaking markets, often paywall contacting people, and don't give bands their own feed cards and tools.
**Secondary:** Facebook groups, Reddit (r/bandmates), local forums and classifieds, Instagram DMs — falls short because: no matching, no filters, no audio-first profiles, posts disappear.
**Indirect (what people mostly do now, per the owner):** Friends of friends, jam nights, bars, music schools — falls short because: slow, limited to your existing circle, and depends on being in the right place at the right time. This is the real habit to beat, more than any app.

## Differentiation
**Key differentiators:**
- Audio first: every visible profile has a clip (people without one are hidden from the feed), so you hear someone before you say hi
- Mutual match: chat opens only when both choose Jam, so nobody gets unwanted messages
- Bands are first-class: bands appear in the feed with open roles; every member approves who joins
- Band tools after the match: rehearsal calendar with Going/Can't, songs (key, tempo, recording, status), setlists, a public band page
- Safety for 16–17 year olds built into the rules, not just the UI
- Free: no subscriptions, no ads; matching and chat stay free
- English and Georgian
**How we do it differently:** The whole path from "who's out there" to "our band's next rehearsal" in one app, built around hearing people play.
**Why that's better:** Fewer wasted rehearsals, faster from match to band, and the band keeps using the app afterwards.
**Why musicians choose us:** To be confirmed with testers.

## Objections
| Objection | Response |
|-----------|----------|
| "Nobody near me uses it yet." | Distance is optional, so the feed can show everyone; early musicians get seen first. Honest risk: with a worldwide launch, feeds in any one city start thin, so marketing should keep inviting friends and bandmates (referrals) and show where people already are. |
| "I don't want to post a recording of myself." | A short phone clip is enough; record right in the app. It's how people decide to say hi, and it saves you bad-fit rehearsals. |
| "Is it safe? (especially for my teenager)" | 16+ only; teens and adults never chat one-on-one; report and block everywhere; moderators review reports. |
| "Is it really free? What's the catch?" | Free, no ads. A small optional subscription may come later for extras; matching and chat stay free. |

**Anti-persona:** People looking to hire a paid wedding band or a music teacher, under-16s, labels or promoters scouting talent, anyone looking for dating.

## Switching Dynamics
**Push:** Dead posts in Facebook groups, no replies, mismatched rehearsals, endless "anyone know a drummer?" messages.
**Pull:** Hearing people before contacting them; matches that are mutual; bands looking for exactly your instrument; it's free.
**Habit:** "I'll just ask around" — friends, jam nights, music schools (the main habit, per the owner); posting in the usual group; existing band chats on WhatsApp, Telegram or Messenger.
**Anxiety:** Empty app in my city, being judged on a clip, safety, another account to keep up.

## Brand Voice
| Attribute | Means | Doesn't mean | Example line |
|-----------|-------|--------------|--------------|
| Direct | Short sentences, plain words, says what happens | Curt or cold | "Swipe. Jam. Form a band." |
| Warm and encouraging | Assumes people want to play together; nudges, never shames | Gushing or cheesy | "Your band isn't looking for drums right now, but you can still say hi." |
| Musician-native | Speaks like a bandmate: jam, rehearsal, setlist | Gear-snob jargon that excludes beginners | "Hear before you swipe." |
| Honest | No hype, no fake numbers, says what isn't there yet | Self-deprecating | "No subscriptions, no ads. Just music." |
| Safe without fear | States the rules calmly | Scary or preachy | "Teens and adults never have private one-on-one chats; they can only meet in bands of three or more." |

**Tone by context:**
- Support: plain, specific, step by step; say what to tap.
- Launch: excited but concrete; show what you can do, not adjectives.
- Errors: say what happened and what to do next; never blame the user.
- Sales: n/a (free app); for store listings and the landing page, lead with the action: hear, Jam, form a band.
- Social: playful, musician humour, short; feature real bands and clips (with permission).
**Terms we own:** Jam / Pass, "Swipe. Jam. Form a band.", "Hear before you swipe", "Find your band".
**Never use (and why):** Dating language (it's not a dating app, and 16–17 year olds use it); "AI-powered" (only avatars use AI, and it isn't the point); invented user counts or reviews.

## Goals
**Business goal:** Grow an active community of musicians worldwide, starting with hobbyists and beginners; reach ~1,000 active users before considering OCTAVA Plus.
**Conversion action:** Install (store) or sign up (web preview) → finish the profile **with at least one clip** (otherwise hidden from the feed) → first match → first band.
**Current metrics:** Pre-launch. Plan: friends test (web preview + APK) → domain → Google Play closed test (12 testers, 14 days) → Supabase Pro at public launch → Apple.
