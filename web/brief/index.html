Marketing brief · week 1

Swipe. Jam. Form a band.

Welcome to OCTAVA! This page explains what we're building, how our weekly tasks work, and what to do first.

What OCTAVA is

A free phone app for finding people to make music with. Musicians make a profile with a short audio clip, then swipe through other musicians: Jam on people they'd like to play with, Pass on the rest. When both choose Jam, it's a match and they can chat. Matches can form a band, and bands show up in the feed with the roles they need, so others can ask to join. Bands then get a rehearsal calendar, songs, setlists and a public band page.

It's for musicians aged 16+, in English and Georgian, everywhere at once. Our first focus is hobbyists and beginners: people who want to play for fun with others at their level.

There are no subscriptions and no ads. One person (the developer) builds it; the marketing plan and weekly tasks are drafted with Claude, an AI assistant that has the whole project in front of it.

Where we are now

The app works end to end, but nobody outside our circle has used it yet. The plan, in order:

  1. Friends test — 5–10 musician friends try it (web preview and Android).
  2. Google Play closed test — Google requires 12 testers for 14 days.
  3. Soft launch — posters with the QR code at jam nights, rehearsal studios, music schools and gear shops; an OCTAVA jam night.
  4. Public launch on Google Play, then iPhone later.

Our channels: TikTok and Instagram Reels, and the local music scene. The biggest risk is an empty feed, so most of the work is about getting musicians to bring their bandmates and friends.

How we work together

  1. Monday: you get this week's tasks, each with a rough time estimate.
  2. During the week: do them in any order. If something is unclear or a better idea comes up, just say so.
  3. Sunday: send a short report using the template below.
  4. The report goes back into the plan, so next week's tasks build on what you found. The real words musicians use are the most valuable thing you can bring back.

The rules

Do
  • Sound like a bandmate: short, warm, plain words. Say musicians, bandmates, Jam, clip, rehearsal.
  • Show real people and real sound, with their permission.
  • Write down exactly what musicians say, word for word.
  • Register any OCTAVA account with the project email, octavabandmate@gmail.com, so the project keeps access.
Don't
  • No dating language (date, flirt, crush): it isn't a dating app, and 16–17 year olds use it.
  • No invented numbers, fake reviews or fake profiles.
  • Don't promise features that don't exist, or say "AI-powered".
  • No content with under-18s without a parent's OK, and nothing aimed at under-16s.
  • Nothing gets posted publicly until the first posts are approved.

Week 1 tasks

About 45 min

1. Try the app like a new musician

Sign up on the web preview, record or upload a clip, swipe through the feed and look at a band. Note the 3 things that confused you and the 3 you liked. (Some profiles there are test musicians for now.)

About 30 min

2. Claim the social accounts

Create TikTok and Instagram accounts with the project email. Try @octava.app first; if it's taken, list 3 available alternatives before choosing. Bio: Swipe. Jam. Form a band. with a link to the landing page. Don't post yet.

About 1.5 hours

3. Map where musicians look for each other

Find 10 online places (Facebook groups, Telegram chats, subreddits, Discord servers) and 5 real places near you (jam nights, studios, music schools, gear shops). For each: link or address, rough size, and whether they allow promotion.

About 1 hour

4. Look at the competition

Spend 20 minutes each on Vampr, BandMix and one local option you know. For each: what they do, what they charge, and 3 complaints from their store reviews, copied word for word.

About 1 hour

5. Sketch 3 short videos

Pick 3 ideas (or your own): "Tinder, but you hear them play first"; "Our drummer quit", where a band finds a new one through the app; "Beginner-friendly", someone who's played for 6 months finding people at their level. For each: the hook (first 2 seconds), the shots, the caption. Ideas only; no filming yet.
